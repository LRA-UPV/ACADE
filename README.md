# ACADE
ACADE project-Development of an open source application for spectrum deconvolution and its validation in liquid scintillation equipment
In radioactivity measurement laboratories, the use of liquid scintillation spectrometers is widespread. They are generally used for the measurement of beta emitters, but also, to a lesser extent, for the determination of alpha emitters. Liquid scintillation spectrometry offers some advantages, such as high efficiency due to its 4π geometry and the possibility of visualizing the spectrum with the peaks of the isotopes present in the sample. However, the low resolution of the spectral peaks, together with the continuous nature of beta emissions, sometimes prevents the correct quantification of the isotopes of interest due to overlap between them. In some cases, this overlap can be avoided by applying a prior radiochemical separation, which usually involves long analysis times and significant costs in materials and reagents, but in other cases separation is impossible because the isotopes belong to the same chemical element (e.g., ⁸⁹Sr and ⁹⁰Sr).

From the perspective of nuclear or radiological emergencies, it is crucial for laboratories to be able to rapidly identify and quantify mixtures of alpha and beta emitters present in contaminated samples. This is also the case in the decommissioning of nuclear facilities, where the number of samples to be processed is very large and analysis times must be optimized, and in the management of waste from NORM industries, where a large number of alpha- and beta-emitting isotopes from the natural decay chains of ²³⁸U and ²³²Th, together with ⁴⁰K, may be present. For this purpose, isotopes present in these types of facilities must be considered, which differ from those usually analyzed by environmental radioactivity laboratories.

One of the best alternatives for the quantification of these mixtures is the application of mathematical methods for spectral deconvolution or separation, enabling their subsequent quantification. For this reason, the research project DEVELOPMENT OF AN OPEN-SOURCE APPLICATION FOR SPECTRAL DECONVOLUTION AND ITS VALIDATION ON LIQUID SCINTILLATION SYSTEMS is proposed. The project is aimed at developing an open-source software application that allows the identification and quantification of the activity of alpha- and beta-emitting isotopes present in liquid scintillation spectra by applying different spectral deconvolution techniques. Alternative techniques will be implemented, such as fitting spectra to discrete functions (Fourier series, Laguerre polynomials, etc.), statistical techniques such as partial least squares regression or functional data analysis, and artificial intelligence techniques such as deep learning. The most suitable technique may be selected depending on the type of sample analyzed. In addition, the application will allow the reconstruction and visualization of individual spectra.

The different methods are being validated through the calibration of the low-background liquid scintillation counter Quantulus 1220 (PerkinElmer) available at the Environmental Radioactivity Laboratory of the Universitat Politècnica de València. Calibration standards for each individual isotope are being prepared and measured, after which the models will be trained using mixtures of these isotopes. Finally, validation will be performed using reference samples or intercomparison samples containing mixtures of alpha and beta emitters with known activity. In addition, these standards and samples will be measured using other types of liquid scintillation detectors, such as Hidex instruments.

The programming language used to implement the application logic is Python, as it is open source, provides a wide range of mathematical and statistical libraries for complex calculations, and allows integration with other programming languages.
